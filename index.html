    What is real and what is simulated

    The readings are generated: 14 days of history plus today, shaped like real hostel, canteen and classroom curves. Everything after that is real logic. A baseline is learned per building and hour, unusual hours are flagged, and the score, chart and illustration all read from the same numbers. A meter feed could replace the generator without touching the rest.

    Assumptions you can change

    • Grid emissions: 0.71 kg CO2e per kWh
    • Landfilled waste: 0.5 kg CO2e per kg
    • Power ₹8 per kWh, water ₹20 per kilolitre
    • One tree absorbs about 21 kg CO2 a year
    • Without monitoring, a fault runs about 12 hours before anyone notices
    • 300 working days a year
    • Carbon credit: 1 tonne CO2e avoided, with a 10% safety buffer withheld
    • Credit value: ₹800 per tonne by default, set with the slider

    How unusual is flagged

    Each hour is compared with that building's 14-day mean and standard deviation. If usage sits more than 3.5σ above the baseline for two hours running, Prakriti raises an alert. That is why a dripping tap at 3 AM is louder than a busy canteen at lunch.

    Plain-language glossary

    Eco-score
    A 0–100 mix of electricity (40%), water (35%) and waste diversion (25%). Higher is greener.
    Typical day
    The average of the last 14 simulated days, hour by hour, for each building.
    Diversion
    Share of waste that never reaches landfill — compost, recycling, e-waste drop.
    Z-score
    How many standard deviations this hour is from the usual pattern. Above 3.5 looks like a fault.
    Carbon credit
    One tonne of CO2e that was avoided. Here they are internal and simulated. A buffer is withheld, and a retired credit cannot be reused.
    Tree-year
    Roughly 21 kg of CO2 absorbed by one mature tree in a year. Used only as a scale, not a planting plan.
    Setpoint
    The temperature you ask the AC to hold. Raising it a degree is one of the cheapest savings on campus.
